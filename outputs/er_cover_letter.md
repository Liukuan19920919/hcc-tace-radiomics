### Summary of the Study

This study developed an interpretable radiomics-based machine learning framework for predicting transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) treatment response and survival in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Using the fully public WAW-TACE dataset (210 patients, portal venous phase CT), we evaluated three classifiers with LASSO feature selection and five-fold cross-validation. The random forest model achieved an AUC of 0.831 ± 0.051, outperforming the clinical baseline by ΔAUC = +0.085. Radiomics-based risk stratification significantly discriminated overall survival (log-rank P < 0.0001) and progression-free survival (P = 0.039).

### Novelty and Significance

We believe this manuscript makes several contributions of interest to the readership of *European Radiology*:

1. **SHAP-based interpretability reveals an underrecognized role for skeletal radiomics.** Our SHAP analysis identified that three of the top ten predictive features derived from skeletal structures (T8 vertebra, ribs 8–12)—a finding that extends the emerging paradigm of opportunistic CT screening into the radiomics domain. This suggests that routine staging CT captures systemic frailty and bone metabolism information relevant to HCC treatment outcomes, without requiring additional imaging.

2. **Systematic comparison of all four CT contrast phases** demonstrates that portal venous phase radiomics significantly outperforms arterial, non-contrast, and delayed phases for TACE response prediction, providing practical guidance for future radiomics study design.

3. **Complete reproducibility through open science.** In contrast to most published radiomics studies that rely on proprietary, single-institution data, every step of our analysis—data acquisition, feature extraction, model training, and SHAP visualization—is fully reproducible using exclusively public data and open-source software. This design explicitly addresses the reproducibility concerns that *European Radiology* and the broader imaging community have increasingly emphasized.
